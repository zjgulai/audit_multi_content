## 1. The test I apply

A clause becomes a candidate only if it can be landed as **an enumerable or regularizable hit expression + a handling action**. That's the whole bar. Two halves:
- **hit_expression** — what string/pattern in the content triggers it;
- **handling_action** — what the system does when it triggers.

Clauses that are plainly statements of intent, definitions, or scope notes don't get forced into candidates — they stay as clause units in the evidence. Clauses that *try* to be rules but can't be landed go to the rejection ledger with a reason code.

## 2. Clause-by-clause

### (1) "no medical claims" → **not determinable as written — rejection ledger**

There is no enumerable hit expression here. "Medical claim" is a *category name*, and this is the classic failure mode: the clause delegates all of its determinability to a vocabulary that isn't attached to it. Two people reading it will disagree on whether "supports milk supply" is a medical claim, and a matcher can't disagree with anyone.

Also worth naming: this category is one where getting the boundary wrong is expensive in both directions — too broad and you block legitimate content, too narrow and you miss a safety-relevant claim.
